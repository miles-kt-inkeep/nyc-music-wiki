# MoMA PS1 Warm Up

Warm Up is the outdoor summer music series held in the courtyard of MoMA PS1, the contemporary-art institution occupying a former public school building at 22-25 Jackson Avenue in [Long Island City](../overview.md). Launched in 1997 and running most summers since, it is the longest-continuously-operating outdoor dance series in New York City, and the event that first established Queens as a credible destination for the city's electronic-music audience rather than a place that audience merely passed through.

## Format

The series runs Saturday afternoons from late June through early September. Sets are booked across experimental electronic music, club-oriented dance, and the outer edges of hip-hop and jazz, with the museum's curatorial staff programming alongside guest selectors. The courtyard is reconfigured each year by the winner of MoMA PS1's Young Architects Program, so the physical room changes annually — a rare case of a venue whose architecture is itself part of the billing.

## Why it matters to this wiki

Warm Up predates the [Ridgewood](../../ridgewood/overview.md) warehouse circuit by nearly two decades. When [Knockdown Center](../../ridgewood/venues/knockdown-center.md), [Nowadays](../../ridgewood/venues/nowadays.md), and [Trans-Pecos](../../ridgewood/venues/trans-pecos.md) opened between 2014 and 2015, they inherited an audience that had already been travelling into Queens on summer weekends for fifteen years. In the frame this wiki uses — Manhattan as the historical center, Brooklyn as the residential present, Queens as the outdoor and warehouse extension — Warm Up is the event that opened the Queens side of that split.

It also sits at the seam between the institutional and the underground. The series is run by a museum, ticketed and permitted, and yet it has consistently booked artists years before they appeared on a Manhattan or Brooklyn bill. That combination is unusual enough that Warm Up is normally discussed alongside [Nowadays](../../ridgewood/venues/nowadays.md) rather than alongside other museum programming.
